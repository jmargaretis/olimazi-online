# Verdict

The honest answer is the one nobody asked for:
    {{I didn’t take on enough risk if anyone were to ask.}}

# Verdict Note

That is not regret about the product. It is the verdict on the position.

# Position Heading

What the position was

# Position Body

Method Effects, 2017 to 2024. **American made. Highest quality. First to market. Already carrying a huge margin.** None of that is the thing that went wrong. It also served as the potential escape from the grueling restaurant industry.

# Position Quote

{{I had an extremely well built, american made, highest quality product(s).}}

# Position Note

A strong hand, stated plainly. The shortfall came from how hard it was played.

# Cost Heading

What it cost

# Cost Body 1

Parts like these start as a nest: shapes laid out on one sheet, then cut. The sheet costs what it costs whether four parts come off it or nine. Everything unused is scrap already paid for. So the real number in the business is not the price of plastic — it is **yield**, parts per sheet.

# Cost Body 2

Only one design was funded. One design nests alone, and alone it leaves the sheet half empty. **Two designs, tooled at the same time, nest into each other** — the gaps one leaves are the shapes the other fills. Same sheet, same cost, far more parts off it.

# Cost Body 3

That was never a design problem. It was a cash problem. Paying for both designs up front is what buys the higher yield, and it only works if they arrive together.

# Cost Quote

{{So if I had my two splitter designs yeild on one sheet of ABS, 2 things would have happened, I would’ve dramitcally increased my already huge margin and generated more cash up front since I was first to market.}}

# Cost Body 4

Two effects, and the second one is the whole story. The margin climbs — that is the obvious half. The half that mattered is **when the money shows up**: more finished product to sell now, out of the same material spend, while the first-to-market window is still open.

# Cost Body 5

And cash up front was the exact thing missing. The business did not fold because the product was wrong or the market said no. It ran out of runway to scale. Which is the point: **the yield problem and the funding problem were one problem.** Spending more at the start was what made the sheet pay for the scale — from the jump, not eventually. That spend was not made.

# Difference Heading

What is different now

# Difference Body 1

# Difference Quote 2

# Difference Note

The cost is named in the same breath as the decision. That is the trade being taken on purpose this time: the slower road, chosen with the price already read out loud.
